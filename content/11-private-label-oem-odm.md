### Article body (Markdown)

Buyers ask "are you OEM, ODM or private label?" as if they had to pick one. They do not. Two of those words describe who designs the garment; the third describes whose name goes on it. You can be all three on a single order, and most first-time brands are.

## The one-sentence version

**OEM** means you bring the design and the factory builds it. **ODM** means the factory already has the block and you adapt it. **Private label** means the product carries your brand, regardless of who designed it.

That is why "private label vs OEM" is not a real choice: a private label jean is still made as OEM or ODM. The label is the branding layer, not the production model.

## What actually changes between them

| Who designs | **OEM:** you · **ODM:** factory block, adapted by you · **Private label:** either, it is a branding choice |
| Tech pack | **OEM:** you supply it · **ODM:** factory supplies, you approve changes · **Private label:** not required for a stock block |
| Pattern ownership | **OEM:** yours after development · **ODM:** factory's base block, your modifications are yours · **Private label:** follows the model underneath |
| Sampling | **OEM:** 10-15 days per style · **ODM:** 10-15 days, faster if the block is close · **Private label:** same as the model underneath |
| MOQ | 150 pcs per style per colour, in all three cases |
| Best for | **OEM:** a designer with a clear fit · **ODM:** a brand testing a category · **Private label:** anyone selling under their own name |

The rows that actually move the price are the first two: how much design work the factory has to do, and who ends up owning the pattern.

## OEM, in practice

You send a tech pack — flat sketches, measurements, fabric, wash, trims, label placement. We build a pattern, sample it in 10–15 days, and send it with a measurement report against your size chart. If the fit is yours, the pattern is yours; you can take it elsewhere later.

The hidden cost of OEM is that a vague tech pack turns into sampling rounds. Each round is a revision cycle, and three rounds cost more than one good tech pack.

## ODM, in practice

We already run blocks for wide-leg, straight, cargo, shorts, long skirt and jacket styles. You pick one, change the fabric, the wash, the hardware, the label and the measurements, and it becomes your product. Sampling is faster because the base construction is proven.

The line to be clear about is ownership: the base block stays with the factory, and the changes you paid for are yours. That is normal in the industry, and it is worth putting in writing before the first order.

## Private label, in practice

Private label is what happens after the production model is settled: your woven labels, your care labels, your hangtags, your polybags and your carton marking. It is a sourcing and packing scope, not a design method.

Two practical notes:

- Labels have lead time. Your branded labels can arrive later than the fabric, so order them at sampling, not at bulk.
- Minimum quantities apply to labels too. 150 pieces is fine for woven labels; small runs of custom printed hangtags often have their own minimum.

## Why the distinction matters to your quote

A factory that quotes the same price for "OEM and ODM" is telling you it has not understood the difference, or that it is quoting a stock block and calling it OEM. A real OEM quote includes pattern making and grading; an ODM quote includes adaptation of an existing block. The two are not interchangeable, and the price difference is the design work.
