Denim Washing Costs: Stone, Enzyme, Laser and Ozone Compared

A denim wash is priced as its own line in a quotation because its cost depends on how many process steps the finish needs, how long it sits in the laundry, and how much hand work it takes — not on the garment it is applied to. Two factories can quote the same jeans at the same fabric price and end up USD 3 apart per piece purely on the wash.

The four finishes, and what each one costs you

Stone washPumice abrasion for a soft hand-feel and a faded, worn look. Water and drum load: high. Hand work: some, for removing stone dust. Cost profile: the highest of the mechanical finishes.
Enzyme washCellulase softens the surface and removes surface fuzz. Water: moderate, cycle time long. Hand work: none. Cost profile: moderate — usually the base layer of a soft finish.
LaserBurns whiskers, honeycomb and graphics into the denim. Water: low, electricity: high. Hand work: low once the file and placement map are approved. Cost profile: moderate to high setup, low per unit at volume.
OzoneLifts shade with ozone gas instead of hypochlorite. Water: low, electricity: high. Hand work: none. Cost profile: moderate — the cheapest route to a clean shade lift.

The table is a cost profile, not a price list. The actual number depends on how these four are combined for your reference, and on how many times the garment goes back into a machine.

What actually drives the wash price

Number of steps. A one-step enzyme wash and a five-step vintage reproduction are different projects. Every additional step means another drum load, another drying cycle and another chance of a defect.

Drying and rework. A wash is not finished when the drum stops. Wet garments are dried, checked and sometimes re-processed. Rework is the most common hidden cost in a vintage brief.

Hand work. Hand sanding, whisker brushes, spotting and localised bleaching are labour, not machine time. They are quoted per placement, per panel or per piece depending on the brief.

Sample development. A wash trial in the sample stage is part of the USD 100 per-style sampling fee. Bulk wash cost is agreed after the standard is sealed, so you are comparing like with like rather than guessing.

How to compare two quotes that both say "washing included"

If wash is buried in the unit price, you cannot tell whether the cheap quote is cheaper or just doing less. Ask three questions:

  • Is the wash quoted as a separate line, with the number of steps named?
  • Is hand work itemised, and is it per piece or per hour?
  • Does the quote include a second wash trial after the first correction, or is that extra?

A supplier who can answer those has a cost model. A supplier who cannot is estimating.

Laser and ozone are not drop-in replacements

They reduce water and they are more repeatable, but they do not reproduce everything:

  • Laser gives you repeatable whiskers and graphics; it does not give you the irregular hand-feel of stone abrasion.
  • Ozone lifts shade evenly and cleanly; it is not a substitute for a vintage reproduction that depends on physical abrasion.
  • Neither replaces hand work where the design depends on placement-level detail.

The sensible brief mixes them: ozone or laser for the base, mechanical or hand processes only where the look genuinely needs them, and a written note on the hand-feel trade-off.

What approval looks like

The approved sample and the sealed wash standard become the production reference. Bulk is checked against that physical sample, not against a photograph. If the fabric weight, the trim or the wash recipe changes, the standard is re-sealed and re-priced — because none of those changes is free.

Buyer questions
Is denim wash included in the unit price?

No. We quote the garment and the wash as separate lines so you can see what the finish costs before committing to a quantity.

Why do factories price wash separately?

Because wash cost is driven by process steps, laundry time and hand work, not by the garment. A separate line is the only way to compare two factories fairly.

Can laser replace stone washing?

No. Laser is repeatable and uses less water for whiskers and graphics, but it cannot reproduce the irregular hand-feel of stone abrasion. Most vintage briefs mix both.

How long does wash development take?

The wash is developed during the 10-15 day sample stage, so you approve a physical standard before bulk starts. A change of fabric, trim or recipe after approval means a new sample and a new standard.
