Denim Shrinkage Explained

Every buyer has had this conversation: the sample fits, the bulk arrives and the same size is short in the leg. That is not a manufacturing error in most cases — it is shrinkage that was never specified. Denim moves after washing, and the only way to control it is to agree the numbers before cutting.

Why denim shrinks at all

Denim is a twill woven from cotton yarns under tension. When the fabric meets water and heat, the yarns relax and the cloth contracts — mostly in the warp (length), less in the weft (width). How much it contracts depends on three things:

  1. Whether the fabric was sanforized or pre-shrunk at the mill. Most production denim is, which brings residual shrinkage down to a few percent.
  2. How many wash steps the garment goes through. A one-step rinse moves less than a five-step vintage wash.
  3. The temperature and mechanical action of the laundry cycle, especially drying.

That is why a shrinkage figure without a wash recipe is meaningless. "3% shrinkage" on a raw fabric and "3% shrinkage" on a stone-washed garment are not the same measurement.

The numbers worth putting in a tech pack

ItemTypical range — Why it matters
Sanforized, garment washed2-3% length, 1-2% width — The normal case for production denim
Sanforized, heavy wash programme3-5% length — More cycles, more movement
Raw or unsanforized5-8% length — Needs a soak-and-measure standard before cutting
Stretch denim (2-4% elastane)2-4% length, 3-5% width — Recovery matters more than shrinkage

These are ranges, not promises. The number that matters is the one measured on your fabric with your wash, which is why we test rather than assume.

How shrinkage is actually measured

The method is simple and unglamorous:

  1. Cut a test garment in the agreed fabric.
  2. Run it through the agreed wash programme — three cycles if the style is expected to be washed repeatedly by the consumer.
  3. Measure the same points before and after: waist, hip, inseam, thigh, hem opening.
  4. Report the percentage change per point, not one blended number.

A single "shrinkage: 3%" line hides the fact that a jean can lose 4% of its inseam and 2% of its waist in the same wash. Those are different failures for the wearer.

Why the sample fits and the bulk does not

Three causes, in order of frequency:

  • The bulk fabric is a different lot. Shrinkage varies between mill lots even on the same article. That is why the shade and the shrinkage standard are sealed together.
  • The sample was cut with extra length as a hedge and never corrected. This works until the first bulk wash.
  • The wash programme changed between sample and bulk — a new machine, a longer cycle, a different dryer setting.

None of these are exotic. All of them are controlled by writing the numbers down and keeping the sealed standard.

What we do on every order

We specify the wash programme as part of the sample, measure the test garment before and after, and record the change per measurement point in the sample report. If the fabric is unsanforized, we soak and measure before cutting the bulk rather than after. The pattern is then adjusted so the finished, washed garment matches your size chart — not the greige garment.

What to put in your tech pack

  • Fabric article and mill, with the sanforized status
  • Wash programme, including the number of cycles you expect the consumer to do
  • After-wash measurements per size, not before-wash
  • A tolerance per point — most buyers allow ±5 mm on the waist and ±10 mm on the inseam
  • Which points are critical: waist and inseam are always; the rest depends on the fit

Buyer questions
Why did my denim shrink after washing?

Denim is woven under tension; water and heat let the yarns relax, so the cloth contracts, mostly in length. Sanforized fabric limits this to a few percent, but the wash programme still moves the garment.

What is an acceptable shrinkage for denim?

2-3% in length for sanforized, garment-washed denim is normal. Raw or unsanforized fabric can move 5-8%, so it needs a soak-and-measure standard before bulk cutting.

Should a tech pack use before-wash or after-wash measurements?

After-wash. The consumer measures a washed garment, so the size chart, the tolerance and the pattern should all be built around the washed measurement.

Can shrinkage be eliminated completely?

Not in cotton denim, but it can be controlled: pre-shrunk fabric, a sealed wash programme, and a pattern compensated for the measured shrinkage.
