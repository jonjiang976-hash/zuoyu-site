Denim Sampling: Timeline, Costs and Why First Samples Come Back Wrong

A first denim sample takes 10-15 days and costs USD 100 per style, credited against the bulk invoice. When a sample comes back wrong, the cause is almost never the sewing floor — it is one of three inputs that arrived incomplete, and each one is fixable before you pay for a second round.

What a sample actually costs

ItemAmount — Notes
Sampling feeUSD 100 per style — Credited against the bulk invoice for the same style
What is includedPattern making, grading, one sample garment, wash development for that garment, measurement report — Photos taken in daylight

The fee is not a profit line. It covers pattern work and a wash trial, both of which happen before anyone knows whether the order will proceed. Crediting it back means the cost of sampling disappears once you order.

The 10-15 days, step by step

  1. Tech pack review (1-2 days). We read the pack for gaps before cutting anything: missing measurements, unstated trims, a wash reference that is a photograph.
  2. Pattern and grading (2-3 days). Base blocks are graded against your size chart; grading increments are confirmed with you first, because a wrong increment is invisible until the sample is on a body.
  3. Cutting and sewing (3-4 days). One garment, but the same marker and the same operators as bulk.
  4. Wash development (2-3 days). The finish is developed against your reference and photographed in daylight.
  5. QC and dispatch (1-2 days). Measurements against the size chart, photographs, packing.

The number that moves most is step 1. A complete tech pack gets you the short end of the range; a pack assembled from screenshots gets you the long end.

Three reasons a first sample comes back

1. The fabric weight differs from the reference. A 10.5 oz comfort-stretch denim does not drape like a 13 oz rigid. If your brief was written from a garment you no longer have, the weight is a guess. Send the garment, or send a swatch with the weight marked.

2. The wash reference was a photograph. Photographs lose contrast and colour temperature. A sample developed from a photo will look plausible on screen and wrong in daylight. The fix is a physical reference: garment, swatch, or a written description of the effect plus a call.

3. Measurements were missing from the tech pack. We can grade a size chart; we cannot invent your fit intention. Bust, waist, hip, rise, inseam and sleeve length are not optional. If you are copying an existing product, the reference garment plus a size chart is faster than a full tech pack.

How to get approval in one round

  • Send the physical reference garment, or a swatch plus photographs of seams and wash detail.
  • Send the size chart, or say explicitly which measurements should come from the reference garment.
  • Say which single feature matters most — the waistband, the wash, the sleeve length. A sample can be right everywhere except one thing and still be unusable; naming the priority prevents that.
  • Comment on measurements, not on impressions. "Waist is 2 cm wide, back rise 1.5 cm short" is a correction we can make. "It feels off" costs another round.

What happens after approval

The approved sample and the sealed wash standard become the production reference. Bulk is checked against them, not against a photograph. If something changes — fabric, trim, wash — we re-quote and re-sample, because the sealed standard stops being valid the moment an input changes.

Buyer questions
How much does a denim sample cost?

USD 100 per style, credited against the bulk invoice for the same style.

How long does sampling take?

10-15 days for a first sample, including wash development and a measurement report, from receipt of a complete tech pack.

Why did my first sample come back wrong?

In practice it is one of three inputs: fabric weight differing from the reference, a wash specified from a photograph instead of a physical reference, or measurements missing from the tech pack.

Can I skip sampling?

We do not recommend it. A sample is the cheapest verification available to a buyer: it resolves fit, wash and workmanship before bulk money is committed.
