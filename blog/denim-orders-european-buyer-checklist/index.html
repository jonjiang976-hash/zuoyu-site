Denim Order Checklist: What EU Buyers Check

A European denim order fails at customs, in a test lab or in a warehouse — rarely at the sewing machine. The five things that decide it are: a REACH-compliant fabric, a responsible person for the GPSR, fibre labels the importer can defend, a wash and fit signed off in writing, and Incoterms that match who pays duty and VAT.

1. The compliance file, not the sample, is what arrives first

Most first orders from China are technically fine and commercially blocked, because the paperwork does not match what the EU now requires.

REACH Annex XVIIWhat it covers: azo dyes (restricted aromatic amines, 30 mg/kg), formaldehyde limits for skin-contact textiles, nickel release from metal trims. Who misses it: a mill that tested the greige cloth but not the washed garment.
EU Textile Labelling Regulation 1007/2011What it covers: fibre composition, tolerances, and the language of the label. Who misses it: a label printed before the final fabric was locked.
GPSR (Regulation 2023/988)What it covers: an EU-based responsible person, traceability, warnings where applicable. Who misses it: any non-EU manufacturer selling direct to EU consumers.
Packaging EPRWhat it covers: Germany's VerpackG (LUCID registration), France's Triman mark, similar schemes elsewhere. Who misses it: importers who registered for one country and ship into five.

Two of these surprise buyers most often.

The GPSR responsible person. Since December 2024, consumer products placed on the EU market generally need a person established in the EU who can be contacted about compliance. If your factory sells to an EU brand, the brand is normally that person. If your factory sells direct to EU consumers, there has to be one — and a Chinese address does not satisfy it. This is not a label you can print yourself.

Packaging EPR. The cardboard, polybag and hangtag have their own producer responsibility in several member states, separate from the garment. Germany's VerpackG registration is the one most often discovered late, usually when a marketplace or a fulfilment centre asks for the LUCID number.

2. Fibre labels: get the numbers right before printing

The label is a legal statement, and the tolerances are tight.

100% cotton rigid denimLabel as: 100% Cotton. Watch out for: mill blends sold as "cotton-rich".
98% cotton / 2% elastaneLabel as: 98% Cotton, 2% Elastane. Watch out for: elastane under 5% still has to be declared in the EU.
Recycled contentLabel as: "20% Recycled Cotton, 80% Cotton" — only if the recycled claim is traceable. GRS is one route, not the only one.
TrimsUsually not required on the fibre label, but buttons and rivets may still fall under the REACH nickel rules.

A practical rule: do not print care or composition labels until the fabric lot is confirmed and the wash approval is signed. Re-labelling 1,500 pieces at the warehouse costs more than the labels ever will.

Care symbols themselves are largely voluntary across the EU, but France expects care instructions and marketplace listings often demand them. ISO 3758 symbols are the safe common language.

3. What gets signed off before bulk starts

The difference between a reorder and a dispute is usually one page of agreed details.

  • Sealed sample. One garment per style, signed and dated, kept by both sides. Not a photo.
  • Wash swatch. The actual approved hand-feel and shade, retained after bulk. "Medium blue" is not a specification; a numbered swatch is.
  • Size chart. Measured on the approved sample, with grading rules written down and a tolerance per measurement.
  • Trim and label pack. Buttons, rivets, zips, main label, care label, hangtag — approved together, with placement marked on a tech pack.
  • Shrinkage and colourfastness results. A tested shrink percentage matters more than a promise, because it decides the size chart.

If a factory cannot show you a signed sample and a retained wash swatch when you ask, that is the answer.

4. Money: who pays what, and where it stops

FOB Shenzhen means the goods are yours once loaded. Everything after that — ocean freight, duty, VAT, last-mile — belongs to the importer, and it is rarely in the unit price the factory quoted.

EU import dutyCotton denim trousers usually sit around 12%. Confirm the exact eight-digit code with a customs broker before you price the landed cost.
VATPaid on import unless postponed VAT accounting is available to you.
OriginChina — no preferential rate for the EU, so duty applies.
DocumentsCommercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, test reports.

An FCA or DAP quote shifts the dividing line, and changes the price by more than most first-time buyers expect. Ask which Incoterm the number in the quotation uses, in writing.

5. A pre-order checklist

Work through this before the deposit leaves your account.

  1. The fabric mill's test report covers the washed garment, not just the greige cloth.
  2. A named EU responsible person exists if you sell to consumers directly.
  3. Packaging EPR registrations are in place for the countries you ship to.
  4. Fibre composition is locked and matches the final fabric lot.
  5. A sealed sample and a retained wash swatch are agreed.
  6. The size chart and grading are written down with tolerances.
  7. The Incoterm in the quotation is named, and duty and VAT are priced in.
  8. Delivery window and destination port are on the purchase order.
  9. Payment terms (30/70 is common) and what triggers the balance.
  10. The test reports you will need are named before production starts, not after.

Missing four or more of these is normal on a first order. Missing the first three is how containers get stuck.

Buyer questions
Is REACH testing required for every denim order into the EU?

REACH restricts substances rather than mandating a certificate per shipment, but importers are expected to hold test evidence for restricted substances such as azo dyes and formaldehyde. Most EU buyers ask for a report before the first order.

Does my Chinese factory need an EU responsible person under the GPSR?

The obligation sits with the economic operator placing the product on the EU market. If you sell to an EU brand, the brand usually takes that role. If you sell direct to EU consumers, a person established in the EU must be named.

Which EU countries require packaging EPR registration for denim?

Germany (VerpackG/LUCID) is the one most often requested, and France requires the Triman marking. Other member states run their own schemes, so check every country you ship into rather than assuming one registration covers all.

Are care labels mandatory in the EU?

Fibre composition labelling is mandatory under Regulation 1007/2011. Care instructions are largely voluntary, but France expects them and most marketplaces require them, so ISO 3758 symbols are the practical default.

What duty applies to denim jeans imported into the EU from China?

Cotton denim trousers typically attract around 12% duty, but the exact rate depends on the eight-digit tariff code and the fabric. Confirm with a customs broker before you price the landed cost.

What should be in the sample sign-off before bulk production?

A sealed signed sample, a retained wash swatch, an agreed size chart with grading and tolerances, and a trim and label pack with placement marked. Photographs alone are not a sign-off.
