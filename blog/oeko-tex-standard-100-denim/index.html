OEKO-TEX Standard 100 for denim: what it covers and what it does not

Buyers routinely treat OEKO-TEX Standard 100 as a general-purpose quality badge. It is a specific product certificate with a specific scope, and knowing that scope is the difference between a clean vendor questionnaire and a rejected one.

Short answer: OEKO-TEX Standard 100 certifies the articles and materials listed on the certificate for restricted substances. It does not certify the company, it says nothing about recycled content, and it says nothing about labour conditions. Those are covered by different schemes: GRS for recycled content, SMETA or amfori BSCI for social audits, ISO 9001 for management systems.

What the certificate covers

Standard 100 is a product-level certification. The certificate names an applicant and lists the article groups and materials it applies to, and it has a validity date. Testing is aimed at harmful substances in the finished textile — the kind of thing a buyer's compliance team asks about when the end product touches skin.

Two practical consequences follow:

  • It is article-specific. Being "OEKO-TEX certified" means the listed articles are certified, not everything a factory can produce. A denim jacket and a denim dress can sit on different certificates if the material or supplier changes.
  • It expires. Certificates are renewed on a cycle. A certificate that lapsed last year is not evidence about today's production.

What it does not cover

Which scheme answers which question
Harmful substances in the garmentOEKO-TEX Standard 100
Recycled content and its chain of custodyGRS / RCS
Labour conditions, health and safety, business ethicsSMETA (Sedex) or amfori BSCI
Quality management systemISO 9001
US product safety for children's / consumer apparelCPSIA-related documents, including a General Certificate of Conformity (GCC)

This is why "we are OEKO-TEX certified" is a poor answer to a question about labour standards. It is not a weak answer — it is an answer to a different question, and a buyer who runs compliance for a living will notice.

How to verify a supplier's certificate in 60 seconds

  1. Ask for the certificate number and the certificate itself, not a logo image on a website.
  2. Check the applicant name against the company named on your invoice and contract.
  3. Check the validity date and whether it covers the articles you are buying — the material composition and article group matter.
  4. Ask what is not covered. A supplier who can list their gaps is easier to verify than one who claims everything.

Buyer questions
Is OEKO-TEX Standard 100 the same as GRS?

No. Standard 100 tests articles for restricted substances. GRS certifies recycled content and its chain of custody. A garment can hold one without the other, and a supplier should be able to show which article is covered by which certificate.

Does OEKO-TEX certify the factory?

No. It is a product-level certificate for the listed articles and materials. Factory labour conditions are covered by social audits such as SMETA or amfori BSCI, and management systems by ISO 9001.

What should I ask a supplier for?

The certificate number, the validity date, the list of covered articles, and confirmation that the applicant name matches the company on your invoice. Ask for the certificate itself rather than a logo on a website.
