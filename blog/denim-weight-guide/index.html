Denim Weight Explained: What 10 oz, 12 oz and 14 oz Change for a Brand

Denim weight is measured in ounces per square yard, and it is the first decision that constrains everything after it: how the garment drapes, how the wash reads, how much it shrinks, and what it costs. For most brands the useful range is 9-14 oz, and the choice should follow the product, not the trend.

The weights we run, and what they are for

WeightHand-feel — Typical use — What to expect
9-10 ozLight, soft, moves with the body — Shirts, tops, dresses, summer jeans — Drapes easily; less structure; watch seam puckering
10.5-12 ozMid-weight, comfortable on first wear — Comfort-stretch jeans, jackets — The workhorse range for womenswear and stretch denim
12-14 ozRigid, structured, holds a crease — Rigid five-pocket jeans, classic truckers — Ages visibly; takes a strong fade; heavier to wear in
14 oz +Stiff, board-like when new — Workwear, raw selvedge repros — Needs a wear-in period; not a mass-market product

Two garments can be the same style and feel like different products because of this number alone. That is why a reference garment with the weight marked is more useful than a photograph of one.

What the weight changes in practice

Drape and silhouette. A 10 oz denim falls close to the body; a 14 oz denim stands away from it. Wide-leg and barrel styles read very differently in each, so the block has to suit the fabric.

Shrinkage. Heavier rigid denim moves more through wash and wear-in than a pre-shrunk stretch fabric. If the finished measurement matters, the shrink behaviour of that specific fabric has to be tested, not assumed from the weight.

How a wash reads. A stone or laser fade on 11 oz looks softer and more even; the same recipe on 14 oz gives stronger contrast because the fabric resists abrasion longer. Buyers who approve a wash on one weight and switch fabric later usually need a second wash trial.

Cost per piece. More fabric per garment and more laundry time both move the price. On our own listings, a 12-14 oz rigid jean sits at the top of the price band (USD 18-26) while a lighter women's style can sit at USD 13.99 — same category, different fabric.

Durability expectations. A 14 oz denim will outlast a 10 oz one in abrasion tests, but customers do not always want that: a lightweight summer jean that ages quickly is a design decision, not a defect.

How to choose the weight for your product

  • Jeans for everyday wear: 11-12.5 oz comfort stretch. It is what most women's denim and much of the men's market actually wants.
  • Rigid five-pocket, vintage look: 12.5-14 oz. Accept the wear-in period; that is the product.
  • Trucker jacket: 11-13 oz. Below 10 oz it reads as a shirt-jacket; above 14 oz it stops moving with the shoulder.
  • Overshirt: 6-8 oz. It needs to sit on top of another garment without bulk.
  • Shirt or dress: 4-8 oz shirting denim. A dress block cut in 12 oz behaves like a different garment.
  • Workwear / raw repro: 14 oz and up. Small volume, specific customer, priced accordingly.

What to send us when you are unsure

A swatch with the weight written on it, or a reference garment we can measure. "Mid-weight" means different things to different mills, so we confirm the weight and the mill before quoting. If the target product needs a weight we do not hold, that is a fabric development conversation, and it is priced before sampling, not after.

Buyer questions
What is denim weight measured in?

Ounces per square yard. 12-14 oz is rigid five-pocket denim; 9-11 oz is comfort-stretch or shirting denim.

Does heavier denim shrink more?

Heavier rigid denim generally moves more through wash and wear-in than a pre-shrunk stretch fabric, but the shrink behaviour depends on the specific fabric. We test the actual fabric rather than assuming from the weight.

Is 14 oz denim too heavy for a mainstream brand?

For a mainstream women's or men's jean, yes — 11-12.5 oz comfort stretch is what most of the market buys. 14 oz suits vintage reproduction, workwear and small run-lines sold on authenticity.

Can you change the fabric weight after I approve the sample?

Yes, but expect a second sample and a second wash trial: the pattern, the drape and the way the wash reads all change with the weight.
