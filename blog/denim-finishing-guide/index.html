Laser, Ozone or Stone Wash? A Buyer's Guide to Denim Finishing

The finish is where most of a denim order's cost and risk sits. Four finishes cover almost everything we run — stone wash, enzyme wash, laser and ozone — and choosing between them is a decision about hand-feel, colour depth, budget and how much you are willing to explain to a compliance team.

The four finishes, in plain terms

FinishWhat it does — Typical use — Watch out for
Stone washPumice stones abrade the garment for a soft hand-feel and faded look — Classic 5-pocket jeans, trucker jackets — Stones wear seams and hardware; heavier handle
Enzyme washCellulase enzymes soften denim and remove surface fuzz — Base for almost any soft-finish brief — Does not create strong contrast on its own
LaserBurns the fade pattern into the denim — Whiskers, honeycomb, placement graphics — Needs a digital file and an approved placement map
OzoneBleaches with ozone gas instead of hypochlorite — Lifting shade with less water — Shade lifting is even, not patchy — plan the look

Rigid denim at 12-14 oz behaves differently from 9-11 oz stretch; the same recipe will not read the same on both. We develop each finish against a physical reference for that order.

How to choose a finish

Start from the look, then work backwards to the process:

  • Soft, clean, uniform — enzyme as the base, stone only if you want visible abrasion.
  • High contrast whiskers and honeycomb — laser for repeatability, hand process only where the laser cannot reach.
  • A lift in shade without a vintage look — ozone, then a short enzyme cycle for hand-feel.
  • Heavy vintage reproduction — stone plus laser plus hand process; expect a longer sample cycle because the reference is physical, not a photograph.

Cost follows the number of process steps, not the number of adjectives in your brief. A one-step enzyme wash and a five-step vintage reproduction are different projects at the same fabric weight.

What buyers get wrong

Specifying a finish from a photo. A photograph flattens a three-dimensional fade. Send the garment if you have it; if you do not, expect the first sample to move the wash toward your reference rather than land on it.

Assuming the wash is included in the unit price. We quote wash separately, on purpose: you should be able to see what the finish costs before you commit to a quantity. The unit price in the category pages is the garment; wash is a line item.

Treating laser as a replacement for everything. Laser gives repeatability and lower water use, but it cannot reproduce the irregular hand-feel of stone abrasion. Skilled buyers mix processes instead of replacing one with another.

Forgetting the compliance question. Some buyers ask for a finish they associate with "sustainable denim" and then discover the claim cannot be supported by documents. We do not hold GRS certification, so we do not describe any finish as recycled or GRS-certified. What we can document is OEKO-TEX Standard 100 for the listed articles, SMETA audited labour practices, and a third-party lab test report for the garment.

How a finish gets approved

  1. You send a reference: a garment, or photographs plus a written description of the effect.
  2. We develop the finish and quote it as a separate line item.
  3. First sample in 10-15 days, with daylight photographs and a measurement report.
  4. You approve against the physical sample; we keep it as the sealed standard.
  5. Bulk runs 30-35 days after approval and deposit; bulk is checked against the sealed standard, not a photo.

The sealed standard is the part that protects both sides. If a bulk wash does not match it, the conversation is about a specific reference instead of an opinion.

Buyer questions
Does laser wash replace hand sanding?

No. Laser is repeatable and uses less water for whiskers, honeycomb and placement graphics, but it cannot reproduce the irregular hand-feel of stone or hand abrasion. Most vintage briefs mix laser with a hand process.

Can you match a wash from a photograph?

We can move toward it. A photograph flattens a three-dimensional fade, so the first sample usually needs a correction round. Sending the physical garment removes most of that risk.

Is wash included in the unit price?

No. Wash is quoted separately as its own line item so you can see what the finish costs before committing to a quantity.
