Private Label vs OEM vs ODM

Buyers ask "are you OEM, ODM or private label?" as if they had to pick one. They do not. Two of those words describe who designs the garment; the third describes whose name goes on it. You can be all three on a single order, and most first-time brands are. The one-sentence version OEM means you bring the design and the factory builds it. ODM means the factory already has the block and you adapt it. Private label means the product carries your brand, regardless of who designed it. That is why "private label vs OEM" is not a real choice: a private label jean is still made as OEM or ODM. The label is the branding layer, not the production model. What actually changes between them

Who designsOEM: you · ODM: factory block, adapted by you · Private label: either, it is a branding choice
Tech packOEM: you supply it · ODM: factory supplies, you approve changes · Private label: not required for a stock block
Pattern ownershipOEM: yours after development · ODM: factory's base block, your modifications are yours · Private label: follows the model underneath
SamplingOEM: 10-15 days per style · ODM: 10-15 days, faster if the block is close · Private label: same as the model underneath
MOQ150 pcs per style per colour, in all three cases
Best forOEM: a designer with a clear fit · ODM: a brand testing a category · Private label: anyone selling under their own name

The rows that actually move the price are the first two: how much design work the factory has to do, and who ends up owning the pattern. OEM, in practice You send a tech pack — flat sketches, measurements, fabric, wash, trims, label placement. We build a pattern, sample it in 10–15 days, and send it with a measurement report against your size chart. If the fit is yours, the pattern is yours; you can take it elsewhere later. The hidden cost of OEM is that a vague tech pack turns into sampling rounds. Each round is a revision cycle, and three rounds cost more than one good tech pack. ODM, in practice We already run blocks for wide-leg, straight, cargo, shorts, long skirt and jacket styles. You pick one, change the fabric, the wash, the hardware, the label and the measurements, and it becomes your product. Sampling is faster because the base construction is proven. The line to be clear about is ownership: the base block stays with the factory, and the changes you paid for are yours. That is normal in the industry, and it is worth putting in writing before the first order. Private label, in practice Private label is what happens after the production model is settled: your woven labels, your care labels, your hangtags, your polybags and your carton marking. It is a sourcing and packing scope, not a design method. Two practical notes: Labels have lead time. Your branded labels can arrive later than the fabric, so order them at sampling, not at bulk. Minimum quantities apply to labels too. 150 pieces is fine for woven labels; small runs of custom printed hangtags often have their own minimum. Why the distinction matters to your quote A factory that quotes the same price for "OEM and ODM" is telling you it has not understood the difference, or that it is quoting a stock block and calling it OEM. A real OEM quote includes pattern making and grading; an ODM quote includes adaptation of an existing block. The two are not interchangeable, and the price difference is the design work. What we do All three, on the same floor: OEM from your tech pack, ODM from our blocks, and private label on top of either. MOQ is 150 pieces per style per colour in every case, sampling is 10–15 days, and the sample fee is credited against the bulk invoice. If you are not sure which model you are buying, describe your starting point — a tech pack, a reference garment, or just a category — and we will tell you which one it is. Buyer questions
Is private label the same as OEM?

No. OEM describes who designs the garment; private label describes whose brand goes on it. A private label product is made as OEM or ODM, then labelled with your brand.

What is the difference between OEM and ODM?

OEM means the factory builds your design from a tech pack. ODM means the factory already has the block and you adapt it with your fabric, wash, hardware and measurements.

Who owns the pattern if the factory develops it?

The base block stays with the factory; the modifications you pay for are yours. This is normal in the industry and should be written into the quotation before the first order.

Does private label change the MOQ?

No. MOQ stays at 150 pieces per style per colour. Custom labels and hangtags may carry their own minimums, but they do not change the garment minimum.
