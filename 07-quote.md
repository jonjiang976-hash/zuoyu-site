How to Read a Denim Factory Quote: 12 Line Items Explained

### Article body (Markdown)

Most denim quotations arrive as one number with "including wash" written under it. That is not a quote, it is an estimate. A real quotation separates the twelve cost lines below, because each one is driven by a decision you made — fabric, wash, hardware, quantity — and each one can be renegotiated separately without moving the others.

## The twelve line items

| 1. Fabric | Weight, composition, shade and mill. The biggest single line, and the one most often quoted as "similar denim". |
| 2. Wash | Process steps, laundry time and hand work, quoted as its own line because it varies per style. |
| 3. Cutting | Marker efficiency, panels per garment and fabric utilisation. Better markers show up here, not in the fabric price. |
| 4. Sewing labour | Operations per garment: a five-pocket jean and a cargo with four extra pockets are different labour, not different fabric. |
| 5. Hardware | Buttons, rivets, zips, D-rings. Branded hardware adds a tooling line. |
| 6. Labels and trims | Woven labels, hangtags, care labels, embroidery. Small per piece, visible per thousand. |
| 7. Lining | Only on coats, long skirts and some dresses; a half-lining is cheaper than a full one. |
| 8. Sampling | The per-style sample fee, and whether it is credited against the bulk invoice. |
| 9. Tooling | One-off cost for custom buttons, rivets or moulds. Should be a separate line, not buried in the unit price. |
| 10. Inspection | AQL inspection, report and third-party inspection if you want one. |
| 11. Packing and cartons | Polybag, carton size, drop test, carton marking. |
| 12. Freight and terms | FOB port, EXW or CIF; payment terms and any currency or banking cost. |

## What each number actually tells you

**Fabric** is the line where a cheap quote hides. Two quotations can both say "12 oz denim" and differ by 30% because one is a mill stock fabric and the other is a specified article with a shade standard. Ask for the mill and the article name.

**Wash** should be broken out. If it is inside the unit price, you cannot tell whether the finish is a one-step enzyme or a five-step vintage reproduction — and that difference is larger than the fabric difference on the same jean.

**Sewing labour** follows operations, not pieces. Every pocket, seam and bartack is time. When a supplier's quote is suspiciously low on a utility style, the missing money is usually in this line.

**Tooling** is one-off and should never appear as a per-piece increase. If a branded button changes the unit price on every piece, the tooling cost has been spread incorrectly.

**Sampling** is a real cost and a real signal. A supplier who credits the sample against bulk is telling you they expect the order; one who does not may be pricing the sample as profit.

## Three questions that separate a quote from an estimate

1. **Is the wash a separate line, and how many steps does it include?** If the answer is a range, you have an estimate.

2. **Is tooling one-off, and is the sample fee credited?** Both are yes-or-no, and both are normally stated in writing.

3. **What is excluded?** A quote that lists only what is included is not finished. Freight, duty, testing, third-party inspection and payment fees are the usual exclusions.

## What changes the number after the quote

A quotation is valid for the specification it was written against. These four changes re-open it:

- **Fabric weight or shade** — a new mill article means a new fabric line and a new shrink test.

- **Wash recipe** — an extra process step is an extra quotation line.

- **Quantity per colour** — the per-colour minimum sits in the fabric and cutting lines.

- **Hardware or labels** — new tooling, new lead time.

### FAQ (optional)

Why is the wash not included in the unit price? | Because wash cost depends on process steps, laundry time and hand work, which vary per style. Keeping it as its own line is the only way to compare two factories fairly.
Should tooling be a separate cost? | Yes. Custom buttons, rivets or moulds are one-off costs; spreading them into the unit price hides them and makes the quote wrong at any quantity other than the one quoted.
What is usually missing from a factory quote? | Freight and duties, third-party inspection, testing, and payment or banking fees. A complete quote either includes them or states that they are excluded.
How long is a denim quotation valid? | It is valid for the specification it was written against. A change of fabric, wash, quantity or hardware re-opens the lines it affects.
